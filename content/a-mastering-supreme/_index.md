## A Mastering Supreme
Ever since I've gone down the neverending audiophile path, I've always wanted to make a place where
I can write down what the best and worst sounding releases there are of a particular album. As a
'budget' audiophile, the cheapest part of the setup to improve is the quality of your music.

The meaning of 'quality' differs between groups of people however. There are three camps that I've
identified so far:

- The **filesize is king** camp are the people that swear they can hear a difference between redbook
	quality and 192kHz 24-bit audio. I used to be in this camp, naively thinking "Qobuz is best" and
	wasting many hours of my life slowly ripping albums from there, only for them to sound just like
	how Spotify sounds. Now, I'm not judging anyone who is like this. If you hear a difference,
	don't let my raving here stop you from enjoying your high-res music.

- The **mastering is king** camp is where I am now. The people here focus more on how the mastering
	or remastering sounds rather than how many bits there are. We lurk the Steve Hoffman forums,
	constantly check the comments of Discogs, before finally deciding to just get all the different
	remasterings.

- The **I don't really care** camp are the non-audiophiles. They can derive full enjoyment from the
	audio quality in Spotify or Youtube. They just listen to the music like normal people. I envy
	them. I wish I could go back to simply listening to the music, and not listening to the minute
	differences between releases and audiophile setups.

Now, there can be an intersect between the filesize and mastering camp. These are the people that
download audiophile vinyl rips in DSD256 or 384kHz 32-bit for some reason. We don't talk about them.

useful (ugh) and not succumbing to the diminishing returns of thousand-dollar headphones and DACs

chain:

ThinkPad E14 Gen 6 > FiiO FT1 Pro

Yes, I don't have a DAC/AMP for my headphones. Yes, I'm driving it solely with the 3.5mm jack of the
laptop. I might not even be a real audiophile to you at this point, but whatever. If I make any
changes to this setup, I'll update this part of the page.

## A Mastering Supreme
Now, this will never be an exhaustive list. If I listen to all the releases there are of an album,
I would probably get sick of it instantly. I don't want that to happen especially since the albums
here are the ones that I absolutely love. Also, the internet sadly doesn't have everything, which
brings us to our next disclaimer: all of these releases are taken from the internet completely free
of charge via some not-so-legal ways. This is for a couple of reasons:

- I don't have money.
- I don't have a ripping setup, either for CDs, SACDs, or vinyl. I shudder just thinking about the
	price that I'll have to pay for a good vinyl setup in the future.
- EVERYTHING that I'll be reviewing is going for exorbitant prices. Say thanks to your fuckass
	extortionist resellers and the labels that decide that these should be limited editions for some
	reason.

I'll probably be limiting my comparisons only to the releases that matter, and only a small amount
of them, maybe 3-5 releases per blog page. These most likely are:

- Audiophile label releases on any medium, e.g. Analogue Productions, MFSL, etc.
- First press vinyl
- First digitization CD
- HDTracks release
- Some other release I can find if I'm not satisfied with how many there are so far

I think I've yapped enough already now. Without further ado, let's put on our listening ears and
start with the mastering comparison supreme!
