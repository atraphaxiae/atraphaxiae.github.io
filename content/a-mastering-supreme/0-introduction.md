## A Mastering Supreme
Ever since I've gone down the bottomless audiophile pit of doom, I've been constantly trying to find
effective ways to upgrade my setup. Headphones, IEMs, DACs, cables, the like. The problem is, these
things take money, and I don't have money. Pretty much everything in the audiophile world, even if
they're 'cheap', aren't actually cheap. The standard for what is inexpensive online is crazy
especially when you come from a not-so-rich country, and a lot of 'budget' audiophile gear is all
but a pipe dream for me.

But there is a way forward for those of us who are not so financially well-endowed, and it takes us
to the very beginning of the audiophile chain: the source music. This is a topic that in my opinion,
many people misunderstand. When I talk about high quality music sources, I'm not talking about
high bitrate or bit depth or whatever, I'm talking about the mastering. You could have a low bitrate
MP3 with a good mastering on it, and it will sound better than any 192 kHz/24-bit file with horrible
mastering.

So how do we get these so called 'good masterings'? Well, you can do it the legal way and buy them.
But that takes money, and as I've said, I don't have money. Or, you can do it the not-so-legal
gray-area way and sail the high seas of the internet. Nothing is ever out of reach in this massive
ocean of bits. Of course, so as to not put heat on myself, I won't be disclosing my methods of
obtaining these media, but I'm sure you can figure it out yourself.

## A Mastering Supreme
Basically, this blog is where I'll be comparing many different releases of albums. This will never
be an exhaustive list; there are just too many releases, especially for popular albums. If I
compared every single release there is under the sun, I would immediately get tired of the album
entirely, and I would be sad because of that. So I'm only going to pick a few, maybe up to six
significant releases per post. I'm not a very consistent person, so don't expect this to have a
consistent schedule.

But before we can dive into comparison supremes, what exactly constitutes a 'good mastering'? For
me, a good mastering is a mastering that sounds good. That's pretty much it. This is a very
opinionated subject, so if I dislike something that you like, it's just my tastes. Take this blog
with a grain of salt is essentially what I'm trying to say. But there are some common things in
masterings I like:

- Each note is 'clear' and 'crisp'
- The sound is 'vertical' and 'open'; listening doesn't feel claustrophobic
- Every instrument is easily identifiable and pinpoint-able
- The sound completely envelops my mind
- Nothing is too hard-panned to the sides, and nothing is too far away
- Everything is never all in the middle, except for mono releases
- There is a minimal amount of distortion, clicks, crackles, etc.
