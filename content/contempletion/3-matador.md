Grant Green is a new and unexpected obsession of mine. My very first experience of him is in Lee
Morgan's *Search for the New Land*, but I honestly didn't think that his role in it was super
significant. I mean, what can you do when Herbie Hancock, Wayne Shorter, and of course, Lee Morgan
are there too. Green felt more like a background character in that album, and he felt pretty
underused in it.

My obsession started when I attended this purely-guitar jazz concert that my friend performed in.
Unfortunately, the arrangements were super short, only around 3-5 minutes long for each track in the
tracklist. Now, when you have ten guitar soloists in the band and only five minutes per track, you
get a very unfulfilling experience where each guitar only solos for a measly few bars. Gosh, most
Post-Bop albums literally only have four dudes in it and they can stretch each track to ten minutes
or even significantly more. That kind of jazz is what I was expecting, and I left the concert pretty
disappointed, but desperately wanting more. I needed a lot more jazz guitar in my life.

I went home, fired up the laptop, and immediately went on the RateYourMusic page for Grant Green.
I'd already listened to his *Idle Moments*, but it isn't a very consistent album; I'd say that the
only truly exceptional piece in it is **Idle Moments** itself. Instead, I found *Matador*. And what
can I say, this album is a masterpiece start to finish. This album has everything; four consistently
incredible pieces, two Blue Note regulars, one-half of John Coltrane's quartet, and another
much-needed rendition of **My Favorite Things**, which is just as excellent as Coltrane's version in
my opinion.

I'd discovered this album just a month ago, and I already felt compelled to write about it. That's
how much I love this. And luckily, PBTHAL also has a Music Matters 33RPM vinyl rip of this album,
though I'd say that it doesn't sound as good compared to other MM remasters I've heard. Not sure why
this is. I love you PBTHAL, and I don't think I can live in this world without you.

- **1.01 Matador**: **5/5**. I love the hook of this piece. Two great solos from Green, and a
	magnificent piano solo from the magnificent McCoy Tyner, complete with a few chord-slammings
	near the end of it. Of course, when you have Tyner, you also have an eternal comping drum solo
	from Elvin Jones, which elevates this piece even further.

- **1.02 My Favorite Things**: **5/5**. My favorite thing in this album, which absolutely gives
	Coltrane's original rendition a run for its money. This is a more energetic affair; not only is
	the tempo faster, you also get Tyner and Jones already going haywire from the very start; you
	can even hear Jones grunting at the intro. Once again, great solos from Green and Tyner. I
	especially love that part in the piano solo where Tyner repeats the same arpeggio over and over
	again across two chords, which is a total eargasmic resolution of tension. As always, you have
	Jones soloing over the entire song in typical Elvin Jones fashion. This piece just makes me feel
	like I can fly, like my entire body is made out of feathers.

- **2.01 Green Jeans**: **5/5**. The most bluesy track in this album, which is far more chill than
	the rest of the album, though that doesn't mean that it's the worst entry here. I'd say that
	this is the best exhibition of Green's Soul Jazz style which he is best known for, and I also
	think the best guitar solos of this album are in this track. Though this is my least favorite
	thing in the album, it's a great piece in a Soul Jazz context. A nice fleeting departure from
	the two modal masterpieces that came before it.

- **2.02 Bedouin**: **5/5**. This track sounds like something you'd hear when you're travelling
	alone across a desert in a western. I think this is the most unique arrangement in this album,
	with its sparse intro and brooding theme. In terms of "music for exploring", this is almost as
	good as Lee Morgan's **Search for the New Land**. Some of my favorite solos from Green and
	Tyner, as well as the drum solo from Jones that I've been longing for since the start of this
	album. Honestly, depending on my mood, I reach more for this song compared to the
	**My Favorite Things** from this album.

*Matador* is an instant album-wide **5/5** for me. People are sleeping on this album; for some
reason it's only rated 3.85 on RateYourMusic, which I don't understand. This album is quite a lot
better than *Idle Moments* if you ask me, and is the more consistently outstanding album, no
contest. Though, I'd say that this album isn't the most "Grant Green-style" album there is, and
*Idle Moments* still is the better starting point for him. But if you're coming from Post-Bop and
want something similar from Grant Green, then look no further. *Matador* is my favorite work from
Green, and in my opinion, probably even his best album.
