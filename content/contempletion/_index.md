## Impermanence and Completionism
I hate the concept of impermanence. I hate the fact that everything I do will be gone and forgotten
someday. In my head it's just: "What's the point?" This one thought has been the source of many
struggles throughout my life. The thought that I'm exerting effort, struggling through the pain, but
in the end, it all just amounts to nothing in some objective cosmic sense.

I've read everywhere that it's not the preservation of the end result, but the experience that makes
it all worth it in the long run. And yes, I think that's true. But it's hard to change my own mind
to think about this instead, especially when hard times are upon me right now. The tunnel vision
that I get from my own suffering is hindering me from seeing the truth that all of this is so that I
can enjoy my future, so that I can finally do the things that I really love.

This entire thing is exacerbated by the fact that I'm a massive completionist. I want to do
everything myself, and finish everything myself. It's like there are two opposing sides of my brain;
one is telling me that there's no point in doing anything, and the other is telling me that I should
be doing everything. I have to finish this one thing before I can do the other thing, otherwise I'm
not doing it properly. But how am I going to do the things, if I can't do one thing because there's
no point in doing it in the first place?

Such privileged first world problems, I know. But it's what I've been going through my entire life,
and it has reached its grubby hands into every aspect of my lifestyle. This section of my website
is one way I've thought of to break this crippling cycle. Basically, this is where I will be listing
down and describing things I've completed. Projects I've finished, games I've 100%-ed, albums I've
listened to, and my thoughts on what I did.

This lets me kill two birds with one stone. I have complete control over this website and its source
code, so it can stay up for as long as I want. I can preserve it as long as I'm alive. This takes
care of the impermanence thing, essentially taking the task of preservation into my own hands. Then,
finishing things finally won't feel so pointless.
