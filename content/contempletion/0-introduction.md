## Impermanence and Completionism
I hate the concept of impermanence. I hate the fact that everything I do will be gone and forgotten
someday. When I think about this, my mind just says: "What's the point?" This one thought has been
the source of many struggles throughout my life. The thought that I'm exerting effort, struggling
through the pain, but in the end, it all just amounts to nothing in some objective cosmic sense.

I've read everywhere that it's not the preservation of the end result, but the experience of it that
makes it all worth it in the long run. And yes, I think that's true. But it's hard to change my own
mind to think about this instead, especially when harder times are upon me right now. The tunnel
vision that I get from my own suffering is hindering me from seeing the truth that all of this is so
that I can enjoy my future, so that I can finally do the things I really love.

This entire thing is exacerbated by the fact that I'm a massive completionist. I want to do
everything myself, and finish everything myself. It's like there are two opposing sides of my brain;
one is telling me that there's no point in doing anything, and the other is telling me that the
point is to do everything. I also have some OCD where I have to do this one thing before I can do
the other thing, because otherwise, I'm not doing it properly. But how am I going to do the things,
if I can't do one thing since there's no point in doing it in the first place?

This brain deadlock is pretty much the sole cause for my issues. Such privileged first world
problems, I know. But it's stuck with me my entire life, and it has reached its grubby hands into
every aspect of my lifestyle. This blog is one solution I've thinked up in an attempt to break this
crippling cycle. Basically, I will be listing down what I've completed, and my thoughts on those
things. Projects I've finished, games I've 100%-ed or plain completed, albums I've listened to,
movies I've watched, etc.

This lets me kill two birds with one stone. I have complete control over this blog and its source
code, so it can pretty much stay up for as long as I want, barring catastrophic user error. I am
essentially taking the responsibility of preservation into my own hands, rather than leaving it to
the destructive whims of other people who don't care and whom I don't trust. This solves the
impermanence problem, at least for the time that I'm here on Earth. Then finally, doing things might
not feel so pointless anymore.
