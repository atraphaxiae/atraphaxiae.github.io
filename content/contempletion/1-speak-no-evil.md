Starting this completion diary off with a review of one of my favorite Post-Bop albums, Wayne
Shorter's *Speak No Evil*. It took me a few relistens to finally get this album. The first time I
listened to this album, it was playing in the background while I was doing map completion for Liyue
in Genshin Impact. I hadn't listened to Wayne Shorter ever before, though I knew he was in Miles
Davis' 60s quintet. At first I didn't understand why this was a 3.93 in RateYourMusic, and I was
pretty disappointed with not being able to grasp this album, especially since the lineup was
unbelievably stacked, with Freddie Hubbard, Herbie Hancock, Ron Carter, and last but certainly not
least, Elvin Jones.

Then one day it finally clicked, and it became one of my favorite jazz albums instantly. This album
contains six perfect pieces, a crazy consistency for an album. It starts with a fanfare from the
energetic **Witch Hunt**, to the haunting beauty of **Dance Cadaverous**, then ends running with the
romantic **Wild Flower**. It's wild how easily Shorter came up with these compositions, not even
deeply thinking about them, then ended up making one of the greatest Post-Bop albums of all time. A
testament to his immense skill as both a composer and a saxophonist.

I watched an interview with Shorter where he called this album 'democratic', and indeed it is. You
can hear it the easiest in the hooks of **Witch Hunt** and **Speak No Evil**, where each phrase is
a conversation between the horns and the piano. The comping of Hancock and Carter is impeccable, and
the relentless triplets of Jones is the cherry on the top end that turns this album into perfection.

This album is simply gorgeous through and through, and I never get tired of it even though I've
probably listened to this a thousand times over the past few years. The Music Matters 33RPM rip by
PBTHAL is a permanent entry in my music library, and is the best way I've found to listen to this
album digitally.

- **1.01 Witch Hunt**: **5/5**. This is in my opinion, one of the best pieces on this album. I love
	the intro fanfare, the way Jones breaks it down before the sax solo, Carter's tone when walking
	his bass into the high notes, and the three incredible solos from Shorter, Hubbard and Hancock.

- **1.02 Fee-Fi-Fo-Fum**: **5/5**. The most traditionally bop entry in this album; not my most
	favorite one here, but this is still a perfect song. The start of the trumpet solo lets you know
	why Freddie Hubbard is one of the GOAT players of his instrument. Still perfect solos from
	the horns and the piano, which is a recurring thing throughout this album.

- **1.03 Dance Cadaverous**: **5/5**. Over time, this has become my favorite track in this album.
	The hook of this piece is my favorite, especially with how silky the horns are. It's been quite
	a long time since I've first listened to this album and I'm still hearing new subtleties with
	the playing in this piece to this day. I think this is the very best example of how elegant and
	unique 'Shorterian' Post-Bop can get.

- **2.01 Speak No Evil**: **5/5**. The title track of this album, which used to be my favorite. I
	absolutely adore how sassy the saxophone solo is on this piece, as well as how behind-the-beat
	and laid-back it is. You also get Jones' most fiery drumming on this track. Honestly, this
	entire album is basically just a 40 minute drum solo with melodies laid on top, and I love every
	second of it. Once again, incredible solos from the horns and the piano, though the saxophone
	solo is the highlight in my opinion.

- **2.02 Infant Eyes**: **5/5**. The only ballad of this album, and a welcome change of pace from
	the constant forward motion pushing you throughout the album. I love the final phrase of the sax
	solo before the piano solo comes in, which is like an intense release of the feeling of longing
	for something. Shorter dedicated this to his newborn daughter, and I think this piece lives up
	to that dedication completely.

- **2.03 Wild Flower**: **5/5**. The most romantic piece, and my second favorite of this album,
	again perfectly showcasing the 'Shorterian' melodies and harmonies that I've come to cherish. To
	me, this piece is the sound of running away with the person you love, acknowledging the
	uncertain future beyond, but still being happy together. There is no better note to end on. The
	perfect end to a perfect album.

Overall, this album gets a **5/5** from me. To be fair, a lot of albums would get a perfect rating
from me; I'm a very lenient rater. But I don't think I've found another album that made me feel the
way this album did when I finally understood it. I'm really happy that I didn't just give up when I
first listened to this album. If you're looking to get into Wayne Shorter's albums, this is his best
album in my opinion, and the best one to start with. You won't be disappointed once you get it.
